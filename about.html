About Sidenote

Sidenote is a study timer with one extra box. While you study, every stray thought (a question, an errand, a worry, an idea) goes into that box in one line, and you keep working. When the block ends, you go through them one by one: a question gets looked up or handed to an AI in one tap, a to-do gets a day or a place in your next block, a worry gets a second look. If you connect an AI (your own key, or the Claude skill), it answers each thought directly. Over time the summary page shows what you worked on, how steady your attention was, and what tends to pull it away.

Why it exists

Most study tools help you with the material. This one is for the moment your mind leaves the material. It started as a simple observation: some of us wander constantly while studying, and trying harder to concentrate doesn't help. What helps is having somewhere to put the thought so it can wait.

Who makes it

Sidenote is designed and maintained by Emma Zhao as an independent student project. The first version of the code was written with the help of an AI coding assistant, and the source is public on GitHub.

How it treats your notes

There is no account. Your thoughts, plans and study history are stored in your own browser. The text of a thought is sent to an AI model only when you ask for that thought to be dealt with. The details are on the privacy page.

What it is not

Sidenote is a study aid. It is not medical or mental-health advice, and its focus score is a rough yardstick for comparing your own sessions, not a measurement of attention. AI replies can be wrong, so check anything that matters.
