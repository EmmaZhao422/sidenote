Can you study with music on? What lyrics do to reading

A Sidenote study guide · Published November 21, 2026

Ask ten students whether music helps them study and you get ten confident answers. The research is less confident than any of them, but it does agree on one thing: what matters most is whether the music has words in it, and whether your task does too.

What the studies found

Three pieces of evidence are worth knowing.

  • Overall, music comes out near zero. In 2011 Juliane Kämpfe and colleagues pooled the studies on background music and adult listeners. Taken together the effect was null. Looked at piece by piece, music disturbed reading and had small negative effects on memory, while it improved mood and helped in sports. The average hid effects that point in opposite directions.
  • For reading, every kind of sound costs a little, and words cost the most. In 2018 Martin Vasilev and colleagues analysed 65 studies of reading with sound in the background. Noise, speech and music each had a small negative effect on reading. Speech you can understand and music with lyrics were the most distracting.
  • Lyrics hurt, lo-fi did not. In a 2023 experiment by Alessandra Souza and Luís Leal Barbosa, college students did memory, reading and arithmetic tasks in silence, with instrumental lo-fi hip-hop, and with songs that had lyrics. Lyrics lowered scores on verbal memory, visual memory and reading comprehension. The instrumental music neither helped nor hurt in any way the data could confirm.

One more detail from that last study: the students knew the lyrics were getting in the way. What they got wrong was the instrumental music, which they sometimes believed was helping when the scores showed no change.

Why words are the problem

Reading, writing and memorising all run on language. So do lyrics. When a song has words, part of you follows them whether you mean to or not, and that is the same part you need for the sentence on the page. A melody without words, or steady rain, doesn't compete for it in the same way. This is the leading explanation, and the 2018 analysis fits it, although it also found some distraction from plain noise, so words are not the whole story.

Tempo and volume matter as well. In a 2012 study by William Thompson and colleagues, music disrupted reading comprehension most when it was both fast and loud.

Choosing for the task

  • Reading, writing, memorising: no lyrics. Silence, steady noise or instrumental music at a low volume.
  • Problem sets and arithmetic: less clear. In the 2023 experiment the effect of lyrics on arithmetic was too small to be sure of. If you want songs anywhere, this is the place to try them.
  • Routine work: copying out notes, formatting, tidying files. Music's lift to mood is real, and there is little here for lyrics to interfere with.
  • A language you don't understand is a reasonable guess at a middle ground, since the words carry no meaning for you. We haven't found a study that settles it, so treat it as something to test on yourself.

The noisy room

Most studies compare music with silence. Many students are choosing between music and a flatmate's phone call. Speech you can understand was among the worst sounds for reading in the 2018 analysis, so covering it with something wordless is a sensible trade. Steady noise or rain does that without adding words of its own. Our guide to background sound goes through the options.

Check it on yourself

Averages don't tell you what you are like. Run a small test: do the same kind of task for a few blocks with your usual playlist, and a few without. At the end of each block, rate how focused you were and note how much you finished. Decide from those notes, since the students in the 2023 study misjudged the instrumental music when they went by feel.

Sidenote's own scenes are rain, waves, fire, wind, a summer night, a train, a quiet library and three colours of noise. None of them has words, and that is deliberate. If you study with your own music playing, pick the Silence scene and the focus scores will still record how those blocks went.
