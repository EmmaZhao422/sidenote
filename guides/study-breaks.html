What to do in a five-minute study break

A Sidenote study guide

Most study advice is about the work. The break gets one line: "then take five minutes." What you do in those five minutes decides whether you come back to the desk, and what state you come back in.

What short breaks do

In 2022 a team led by Patricia Albulescu pooled 22 study samples (2,335 people) on "micro-breaks", meaning breaks of ten minutes or less between tasks. Two results were clear and one was not.

  • People who took short breaks felt more energetic afterwards and less tired. Both effects were small but reliable.
  • Performance on the next task did not improve on average. Within that ten-minute limit, the longer the break, the more it helped performance, and the authors suggest that recovering from very demanding work may need more than ten minutes.

So a five-minute break is mainly for how you feel, and that matters, because how you feel decides whether you start the next block. After a long, hard stretch, take a longer one.

A break that ends on time

  1. Decide the length before it starts, and put it on a timer. A break with no end drifts.
  2. Stand up and leave the desk. Walk to the kitchen, fill a glass of water, stretch your back. If you stay in the chair, it doesn't feel like a break.
  3. Look at something far away. Out of a window if you have one. Your eyes have been focused at arm's length the whole block.
  4. Deal with one parked thought. A two-minute errand from your list is a good use of a break: you rest from the material and the list gets shorter.
  5. Come back when the timer goes, and start with something easy. Re-read your last line, or tick off a step. The first thirty seconds back are the hard part.

What turns five minutes into forty

A feed has no natural end, so a five-minute scroll rarely stays five minutes. If the phone is your break, use it for something that finishes: answer one message, play one song. Keep the feed for the long break at the end of the day, when it can't take the study time. More on this in where your phone should be while you study.

How often

No single schedule is right for everyone. A common pattern is five minutes after a 25-minute block and fifteen to twenty after three or four blocks. If you work in 45- or 60-minute blocks, take ten.
