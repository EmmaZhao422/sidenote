Study guides

Short pieces on the ideas behind Sidenote. Each takes under five minutes to read, and you can use it the same day.

  • The parking lot method: what to do with a thought that shows up mid-study
    Why stray thoughts keep interrupting your studying, and a simple way to write them down and deal with them after the block ends.
  • Five minutes before you study: a short plan beats a long warm-up
    A five-minute routine for starting a study session: name the goal, split it into steps, empty your head, and settle in.
  • Rain, brown noise or silence: finding the sound you focus with
    What research says about studying with background sound, why it differs from person to person, and how to test it on yourself.
  • 25 minutes or 45? How long a study block should be
    What studies of the Pomodoro technique found about fixed study blocks and breaks, and how to pick a block length from your own data.
  • Where should your phone be while you study?
    The "brain drain" study said a phone on the desk hurts your thinking even when you ignore it. Later studies disagree. Here is what holds up, and what to do.
  • Why your mind wanders while you study, and what to do instead of fighting it
    Minds wander almost half the time, during nearly everything. What that means for studying, and three things that work better than trying harder.
  • A worry showed up while you were studying. Give it an appointment.
    How to handle a worry that arrives mid-study: write it down, give it a time later today, and what the research on "worry time" does and does not show.
  • What to do in a five-minute study break
    Short breaks leave you less tired. What a 2022 review of 22 studies found, and how to take a break that ends when you meant it to.
  • Re-reading feels like studying. Testing yourself works better.
    A review of ten common study techniques rated self-testing and spreading sessions out as the most useful, and highlighting and re-reading as the least. How to use that in a 25-minute block.
  • One study block, start to finish: a worked example
    A 45-minute block followed minute by minute: the plan, the five stray thoughts that showed up, what was done with each one afterwards, and what the record showed.
